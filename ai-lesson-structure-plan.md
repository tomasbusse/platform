# AI Lesson Generator Structure Plan

## Simmonds Language School - Lesson Framework

---

## Core Principle

> **Text = Visual support/instructions | Audio = Listening content/examples | Images = Context & comprehension aids**

The audio should NEVER simply read the slide. Each element serves a distinct pedagogical purpose.

---

## Lesson Structure (10-12 Slides)

---

### Slide 1: Lesson Introduction

| Element | Purpose | Content |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Text** | Topic & objectives | "Today's Topic: [Topic]" + 2-3 learning goals |
| **Audio** | Warm-up question/engagement | Conversational hook, relate to student's life, preview what they'll learn |
| **Image Prompt** | Set the scene | "[Scene description], realistic photography style, [lighting/mood]" |

**Example:**
- **Text:** "Today's Topic: Ordering Food at a Restaurant" + Learning goals
- **Audio:** "Have you ever been to a restaurant in an English-speaking country? What was your experience like? Today we're going to learn how to confidently order food."
- **Image Prompt:** "Interior of a modern casual restaurant with warm lighting, empty table with menu, realistic photography style, soft focus background"

---

### Slide 2: Vocabulary Introduction

| Element | Purpose | Content |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Text** | Key vocabulary list (5-7 words) | Words with translations/definitions |
| **Audio** | Pronunciation + example sentences | Model each word, provide context sentences, natural pace |
| **Image Prompt** | Visual vocabulary support | Relevant images showing vocabulary items |

**Example:**
- **Text:** appetizer, main course, side dish, bill, tip, reservation, specials
- **Audio:** "Let's learn some key words. First: 'appetizer' - an appetizer is a small dish before your main meal. For example: 'I'd like to order an appetizer while we wait.'"
- **Image Prompt:** "Flat lay photo of restaurant appetizers on white plates, bruschetta, soup, salad, professional food photography, natural lighting"

---

### Slide 3: Listening - Dialogue Part 1

| Element | Purpose | Content |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Text** | Comprehension questions (pre-listening) | 2-3 questions to focus listening |
| **Audio** | Full dialogue (THE listening exercise) | Natural conversation at appropriate speed |
| **Image Prompt** | Scene visualisation | People in the dialogue situation |

**Example:**
- **Text:** "Listen and answer: 1. What does the customer order to drink? 2. Does she want an appetizer?"
- **Audio:** *Waiter: "Good evening, welcome to Antonio's. Can I get you something to drink?" Customer: "Yes, I'd like a glass of sparkling water, please." Waiter: "Of course. Would you like to see the appetizer menu?"...*
- **Image Prompt:** "Waiter in black apron taking order from female customer at restaurant table, holding notepad, realistic photo, evening restaurant ambiance"

---

### Slide 4: Comprehension Check

| Element | Purpose | Content |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Text** | Answers + key phrases highlighted | Correct answers + useful phrases box |
| **Audio** | Explanation & repetition drills | Review answers, highlight language, practice pronunciation |
| **Image Prompt** | Focus on key item from dialogue | Close-up relevant to answers |

**Example:**
- **Text:** Answers + Useful phrases: "I'd like...", "Can I get...", "Would you like...?"
- **Audio:** "Let's check your answers. The customer ordered sparkling water. Notice how she said 'I'd like' - this is a polite way to order. Repeat after me: 'I'd like a glass of sparkling water.'"
- **Image Prompt:** "Glass of sparkling water with ice and lemon slice on restaurant table, close-up, natural lighting, photorealistic"

---

### Slide 5: Language Focus - Grammar/Phrases

| Element | Purpose | Content |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Text** | Grammar structure or phrase pattern | Clear formula/pattern |
| **Audio** | Multiple examples with variations | Explain rule, give many examples, model usage |
| **Image Prompt** | Supportive context image | Related to the language being taught |

**Example:**
- **Text:** "Polite Ordering: I'd like + [noun] / Could I have + [noun]?"
- **Audio:** "When ordering, we use 'I'd like' or 'Could I have'. Listen to these examples: 'I'd like the chicken, please.' 'Could I have the bill?' 'I'd like to order dessert.' Now you know two polite ways to ask for things."
- **Image Prompt:** "Person pointing at restaurant menu while talking to waiter, over-shoulder view, realistic, casual dining setting"

---

### Slide 6: Listening - Dialogue Part 2

| Element | Purpose | Content |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Text** | New comprehension questions | Questions about new dialogue section |
| **Audio** | Continuation of dialogue | Next part of the story/conversation |
| **Image Prompt** | New scene from dialogue | Reflects what happens in this section |

**Example:**
- **Text:** "Listen: 1. What problem does the customer have? 2. How does the waiter solve it?"
- **Audio:** *Customer: "Excuse me, I'm afraid this isn't what I ordered. I asked for the grilled salmon, not the fried fish." Waiter: "I'm so sorry about that. Let me take this back and bring you the correct dish right away."...*
- **Image Prompt:** "Customer at restaurant table looking concerned while speaking to attentive waiter, plate of food on table, realistic photography"

---

### Slide 7: Useful Expressions - Problems & Solutions

| Element | Purpose | Content |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Text** | Phrase bank for specific situations | Categorised useful phrases |
| **Audio** | Pronunciation drill + context explanations | When and how to use each phrase |
| **Image Prompt** | Relevant scenario | Visual support for the situation |

**Example:**
- **Text:** "Polite Complaints: I'm afraid... / This isn't what I ordered / Could you please...?"
- **Audio:** "Sometimes things go wrong. Here's how to politely fix problems. Repeat: 'I'm afraid this isn't what I ordered.' Good. Now: 'Could you please bring me a clean fork?' These phrases are polite but clear."
- **Image Prompt:** "Close-up of two different fish dishes side by side on restaurant table, one grilled salmon one fried fish, comparison, food photography"

---

### Slide 8: Practice - Gap Fill or Matching

| Element | Purpose | Content |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Text** | Interactive exercise | Gap-fill, matching, or multiple choice |
| **Audio** | Instructions + hints/guidance | Explain the task, offer support |
| **Image Prompt** | Study/learning context | Someone studying or working on exercises |

**Example:**
- **Text:** Gap-fill dialogue with missing phrases
- **Audio:** "Now let's practice. Look at the dialogue and fill in the missing words. Use the phrases we've learned. I'll give you a hint for number one: the customer is ordering politely..."
- **Image Prompt:** "Person's hands writing in notebook at cafe table with coffee cup, study materials, warm natural lighting, realistic"

---

### Slide 9: Speaking Practice

| Element | Purpose | Content |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Text** | Role-play scenario + prompt cards | Clear roles and objectives |
| **Audio** | Model example + encouragement | Demonstrate then encourage student production |
| **Image Prompt** | Two people in conversation | Models the speaking situation |

**Example:**
- **Text:** "Role A: Customer - order a main course and drink. Role B: Waiter - take the order, ask about sides."
- **Audio:** "Now it's your turn to practice. Listen to an example first... [short model]. Now pause the lesson and practice with a partner, or try speaking both roles yourself. Remember to use 'I'd like' and 'Could I have'."
- **Image Prompt:** "Two business people having conversation at restaurant table, one holding menu, natural expressions, realistic professional photo"

---

### Slide 10: Cultural Tip

| Element | Purpose | Content |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Text** | Brief cultural note | Key points about culture/etiquette |
| **Audio** | Expanded cultural explanation | Detailed context and advice |
| **Image Prompt** | Cultural relevance | Image related to cultural point |

**Example:**
- **Text:** "Tipping in English-Speaking Countries" + comparison table
- **Audio:** "Here's something important about restaurant culture. In the US, tipping 15-20% is expected. In the UK, it's usually 10-12% and often optional. In Australia, tipping isn't expected but appreciated. Always check if service is included in your bill."
- **Image Prompt:** "Restaurant bill on small tray with credit card and cash tip, wooden table, overhead view, realistic photo"

---

### Slide 11: Summary & Key Takeaways

| Element | Purpose | Content |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Text** | Bullet points of main learning | 5-6 key phrases/structures |
| **Audio** | Quick review + final practice | Consolidate learning, quick drills |
| **Image Prompt** | Positive conclusion | Successful completion of activity |

**Example:**
- **Text:** Key phrases learned today (listed)
- **Audio:** "Great work today! Let's review. To order politely, say 'I'd like' or 'Could I have'. If there's a problem, start with 'I'm afraid' or 'Excuse me'. Can you remember how to ask for the bill? That's right: 'Could I have the bill, please?'"
- **Image Prompt:** "Happy customer leaving restaurant, waving goodbye to staff at door, evening city lights visible, realistic, warm atmosphere"

---

### Slide 12: Homework/Next Steps

| Element | Purpose | Content |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Text** | Assignment + preview of next lesson | Clear homework task |
| **Audio** | Motivation + detailed instructions | Explain task, encourage practice |
| **Image Prompt** | Forward-looking | Preview of next topic |

**Example:**
- **Text:** "Homework: Write a short restaurant dialogue (8-10 lines). Next lesson: Shopping for Clothes"
- **Audio:** "Before our next lesson, try writing your own restaurant dialogue. Include ordering, asking a question, and paying the bill. If you can, try practicing at a real restaurant! Next time, we'll learn how to shop for clothes in English. See you then!"
- **Image Prompt:** "Clothing store interior with racks of clothes, modern boutique, natural lighting, realistic retail environment"

## Content Generation Prompts

### Master Prompt for AI Lesson Generation

```
Generate a language lesson on [TOPIC] for [LEVEL] students.

For each slide, provide:
1. SLIDE TEXT: What appears on screen (concise, visual support only)
2. AUDIO SCRIPT: What ElevenLabs will speak (conversational teaching tone, includes examples, pronunciation guidance, and engagement)
3. IMAGE PROMPT: Realistic photography style prompt for AI image generation

CRITICAL RULES:
- Audio must NEVER simply read the slide text
- Audio is the TEACHING element (explanations, examples, drills, engagement)
- Text is the REFERENCE element (vocabulary, structures, questions, answers)
- Images provide CONTEXT and COMPREHENSION support
- All image prompts must specify "realistic photography" - no cartoons or illustrations
```

---

### Image Prompt Formula

```
[Subject/scene] + [specific details] + [setting/environment] + "realistic photography" + [lighting/mood] + [composition if needed]

ALWAYS INCLUDE: realistic, photorealistic, natural lighting, professional photo
NEVER INCLUDE: cartoon, illustration, anime, artistic, painting, drawing, clipart
```

**Examples:**
- ✅ "Business meeting in modern office, four professionals around table with laptops, natural lighting, realistic photography"
- ✅ "Close-up of hands typing on laptop keyboard, coffee cup nearby, home office setting, soft natural light, photorealistic"
- ❌ "Cartoon illustration of business meeting"
- ❌ "Artistic rendering of office scene"

## Audio Script Guidelines

| Slide Type | Audio Purpose | Tone | Length |
|------------|--------------|------|--------|
| Introduction | Engage & preview | Warm, enthusiastic | 20-30 sec |
| Vocabulary | Pronounce & contextualise | Clear, measured pace | 45-60 sec |
| Listening | BE the content | Natural dialogue speed | 30-60 sec |
| Comprehension | Review & drill | Teacher-like, encouraging | 30-45 sec |
| Grammar | Explain & model | Clear, patient | 45-60 sec |
| Practice | Instruct & support | Supportive, patient | 20-30 sec |
| Cultural | Inform & advise | Conversational, interesting | 30-45 sec |
| Summary | Reinforce & celebrate | Upbeat, congratulatory | 30-45 sec |

---

## Audio Script Best Practices

### DO:
- Use natural, conversational language
- Include pauses for student repetition: "Repeat after me: [phrase]. Good!"
- Ask rhetorical questions to engage: "Have you ever...?"
- Provide multiple examples of each structure
- Give pronunciation tips: "Notice how the 't' sounds like a 'd' here..."
- Encourage and praise: "Great work!", "Well done!"

### DON'T:
- Simply read what's on the slide
- Use overly formal or written language
- Speak too fast for the level
- Forget to include example sentences
- Skip pronunciation modelling
- Sound robotic or monotone (write for natural TTS output)

---

## Slide Text Best Practices

### DO:
- Keep text minimal and scannable
- Use clear headings
- Highlight key phrases in boxes
- Present vocabulary in clean lists
- Make questions clear and numbered
- Use visual hierarchy

### DON'T:
- Write paragraphs
- Include everything the audio says
- Clutter the slide
- Use small fonts (keep it readable)
- Forget to include answers on check slides

## Level Adjustments

| Level | Vocabulary | Audio Speed | Grammar Complexity | Dialogue Length |
|-------|------------|-------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| A1 | 5-6 basic words | Slow, clear | Simple present, basic phrases | 4-6 exchanges |
| A2 | 6-7 words | Measured | Past simple, common structures | 6-8 exchanges |
| B1 | 7-8 words | Natural pace | Mixed tenses, conditionals | 8-10 exchanges |
| B2 | 8-10 words | Natural | Complex structures, idioms | 10-12 exchanges |
| C1 | 10-12 words | Fast natural | Advanced grammar, nuance | 12+ exchanges |
